# Attempt Comparison: Physical Measurements vs. Model Predictions vs. Validated Improvement

## 1. Context & Purpose
In VERBAMIND, users can practise multiple attempts (retakes) of the same exercise and compare their physical acoustic delivery metrics side by side.

When building speech training systems, there is an omnipresent temptation to label every numeric change as "better" or "worse", or to summarize differences into an arbitrary "fluency score". This document articulates the scientific and pedagogical rationale for why VERBAMIND strictly rejects pseudo-evaluative labeling in favor of objective, evidence-linked physical comparison.

---

## 2. The Three Distinct Epistemic Layers

To maintain academic and scientific integrity, VERBAMIND categorizes system outputs into three strictly separated tiers:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 1: Deterministic Physical Acoustic Measurements                   |
| (Sample rate, frame RMS energy, speech active time, gap duration)       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                    |
                                    v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 2: Probabilistic Model Predictions                                |
| (Whisper ASR lexical text, word-level attention timestamps, WPM)        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                    |
                                    v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 3: Validated Pedagogical Improvement                              |
| (Communicative effectiveness, rhetorical clarity, listener impression)  |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Layer 1: Deterministic Physical Acoustic Measurements
- **What they are**: Direct algorithmic calculations derived from raw audio waveforms (e.g. 16 kHz mono float32 samples).
- **Examples**:
  - Total recording duration ($\pm 0.01\text{ s}$)
  - Speech-active duration ($T_{\text{speech}}$, frames where $\text{RMS} \ge -40\text{ dBFS}$)
  - Speech activity ratio ($T_{\text{speech}} / T_{\text{total}} \times 100\%$)
  - Internal silent gaps count and duration ($\ge 250\text{ ms}$)
- **Epistemic Status**: Falsifiable, reproducible, objective physical properties of the sound wave. They contain zero assumptions about language, intent, or quality.

### Layer 2: Probabilistic Model Predictions
- **What they are**: Statistical inferences generated by machine learning models trained on large corpora.
- **Examples**:
  - ASR lexical transcript (CTranslate2 Whisper `base.en`)
  - Word start and end boundaries (approximate cross-attention weights)
  - Speaking pace ($\text{WPM} = \text{word count} / \text{duration} \times 60$)
- **Epistemic Status**: Probabilistic approximations. Model predictions can suffer from substitution errors (e.g., proper nouns), hallucination, or approximate boundary smearing.
- **Handling**: These are explicitly labeled as `rate_is_transcript_dependent` and marked as "Approximate Alignment". If a transcript fails or is unavailable, acoustic measurements remain fully valid, while WPM displays `N/A (Transcript required)`.

### Layer 3: Validated Pedagogical Improvement
- **What it is**: The degree to which a speaker's communication meets human communicative goals (e.g. clarity, persuasiveness, rhetorical impact, comfort).
- **The Fallacy of Automatic Value Judgments**:
  - *Faster is not necessarily better*: A speaker who rushes through an interview response at 180 WPM may sound panicked and unreflective; reducing pace to 135 WPM is an improvement, yet $\Delta\text{WPM}$ is negative.
  - *Fewer pauses are not necessarily better*: Pauses provide vital cognitive pacing for the listener, demarcate syntactic clauses, and emphasize key arguments. Eliminating natural pauses produces an unnatural, breathless delivery.
  - *Higher speech activity is not necessarily better*: 98% speech activity without breathing pauses impairs comprehension.
- **Handling in VERBAMIND**:
  - The UI displays signed deltas with physical units ($\pm\text{s}$, $\pm\text{gaps}$, $\pm\text{WPM}$).
  - The UI **NEVER** colors negative or positive deltas as green (good) or red (bad).
  - The system provides **zero** fake composite scores (e.g., "Fluency: 84/100").
  - The UI features an explicit disclaimer explaining that communicative improvement is contextual and cannot be deduced from speed or pause reduction alone.

---

## 3. Comparison Compatibility Rules

Valid comparison requires that both recordings be captured and analyzed under identical constraints:
1. **Exercise Matching**: Attempts of different exercises cannot be compared.
2. **Detector Sensitivity**: Energy thresholds (-40.0 dBFS) and minimum pause durations (0.25s) must match.
3. **Recognizer Model**: Transcripts generated by different models (e.g. `base.en` vs `small.en`) must flag potential alignment differences.

When any configuration diverges, VERBAMIND renders an explicit **Compatibility Warning Banner** detailing the exact parameters that differ.

---

## 4. Single-Player Invariant & Resource Lifecycle

1. **One Active Audio Element**: The interface maintains exactly one `<audio>` element in the DOM. Switching between Current and Baseline takes switches the audio source, guaranteeing that overlapping playback cannot occur.
2. **Object URL Lifecycle**: Retaining an attempt creates a dedicated Blob Object URL (`URL.createObjectURL(blob)`), ensuring that resetting the recorder for Take 2 does not invalidate Take 1's audio stream. When the baseline is cleared or replaced, `URL.revokeObjectURL` is immediately invoked to prevent memory leaks in long browser sessions.
3. **Session-Only Memory**: In accordance with user privacy invariants and current milestone scope, comparison data resides strictly in volatile browser memory and clears completely upon page reload.
